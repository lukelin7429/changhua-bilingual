The five offices 五處室

Almost every question you have in your first month belongs to one of these five. Walk in and ask — that is what they are there for.

教務處 Academic Affairs

Timetables, textbooks, exams, the school calendar, booking the English classroom.

Ask for: 教學組長 Curriculum Section Chief
學務處 Student Affairs

Student safety and health, discipline, assemblies, sports day, clubs, injuries.

Ask for: 生教組長 or the school nurse
輔導室 Counseling

Student counseling, special educational needs, referrals to social services.

Raising a concern here is never an overreaction.
總務處 General Affairs

Keys, repairs, equipment, purchasing, anything about the building.

Ask for: 事務組
人事室 Personnel

Your contract, leave forms, health insurance, work permit paperwork.

Learn this person's name in Week 1.

The hierarchy 職稱

校長 Principal → 主任 Director, heads an office → 組長 Section Chief, heads a section inside it → 老師 Teacher. Using “王主任” or “李組長” shows you understand the structure — but calling anyone 老師 is never wrong or rude.

Your three key people

  • Personnel Officer人事
    Everything about your status — contract, leave, insurance, work permit, renewal.
  • Your co-teacher協同教師
    Everything about your classes — planning, materials, what happened in the room.
  • The homeroom teacher導師
    Everything about a particular student — level, family, friendships, absence. They stay with the same class for years and are the family's main contact. Go to them before you go to any office.

The one red line

Do not get this wrong

No outside teaching work without a separate permit

Your work permit authorizes you to work for one employer. Taking Saturday hours at a cram school without a permit for that employer breaches the Employment Service Act: a fine of NT$30,000–150,000, and the authorities may revoke your permit and order you to leave.

Your principal cannot authorize it. Declaring the income does not fix it — the offense is working without authorization, not failing to pay tax. This is the most common way a foreign teacher loses their position in Taiwan, and it is almost always avoidable.

One thing to do this week

Ask 教務處 for the school calendar (行事曆) and put every date in your phone — especially the make-up school days (補課日), which are working Saturdays that compensate for extended holidays. Turning up to an empty school, or not turning up to a full one, is a first-year mistake you only need to avoid once.
