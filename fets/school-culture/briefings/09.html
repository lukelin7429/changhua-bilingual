The regulation

Student assessment in elementary and junior high schools is governed by the Regulations on Student Learning Assessment for Elementary and Junior High Schools (國民小學及國民中學學生學習評量辦法, as amended 24 April 2024). It is short, and three of its articles change how you should think about your own contribution.

Three kinds of assessment — and where you come in

MethodWhat it covers
紙筆測驗及表單
Written tests and forms		Knowledge and concepts, plus attitude and motivation: worksheets, homework, written tests, questionnaires, checklists, rating scales.
實作評量
Performance assessment		Problem-solving, skills, participation and conduct: written reports, oral reports, listening and oral communication, practical operation, making things, performance, appreciation, observed behavior.
檔案評量
Portfolio assessment		A systematically assembled file of forms, tests, performance assessments and other records, showing the learning process and its results.

Read the middle row again. “Listening and oral communication” is named in the regulation as a form of assessment — and it is the one thing in that list you are better placed to judge than anyone else in the building. Your contribution is not a nice extra sitting outside the system; it is a category the system already recognises.

Why the exam calendar looks the way it does

Assessment is split into 平時評量 (ongoing) and 定期評量 (periodic). For written tests the periodic kind is capped: at most twice a semester in grades 1–2, and at most three times a semester in grades 3–9.

That cap is why 段考 weeks are so concentrated and so disruptive — a whole semester of formal written assessment is squeezed into two or three windows. Module 2 told you to plan your units around them; this is the rule underneath that advice.

How results are written down

ProvisionWhat it means
RecordingResults are recorded as a grade band, a number, or a qualitative written description (等第、數量或質性文字描述). A sentence about what a child can now do is a legitimate result, not a soft substitute for one.
The bandsDomain courses use 優 / 甲 / 乙 / 丙 / 丁, and 丙 is the pass. A 丙 is not a bad grade — it is the line the regulation draws.
GraduationAttendance of at least two-thirds of the required days after approved absences are deducted, no more than three major demerits, and an average of 丙 or above in at least four learning domains.
The one that surprises people

Schools may publish how the scores were distributed. They may never publish where an individual student ranked.

A school can announce that the class average was 78, or how many students fell in each band. It cannot publicly show that this child was 7th in the class or 143rd in the year.

If you have taught somewhere that posts rank lists, this is the sharpest break from what you are used to. It also means you cannot answer the question a parent will eventually ask you directly — see below.

Your actual position

  • You contribute, you do not issue提供，而非核定
    Whatever you assess goes into the school's system through your co-teacher or 教務處, in the form they ask for. Do not keep a parallel grade of your own and hand it to anyone directly.
  • Ask early what form they want先問格式
    A band? A number out of ten? Three sentences? Schools differ, and the answer changes how you should have been recording things all semester. Ask in September, not in June.
  • Write what a child can do質性描述
    Qualitative description is an accepted result. “Can introduce herself and ask three follow-up questions without prompting” is more useful to the next teacher than 甲, and the regulation allows it.
  • Never negotiate a grade with a student or parent
    If someone challenges an assessment, it goes through the school's process. Adjusting a result because you were asked to is the fastest way to make every future result negotiable.

If you are staying

Raise renewal with the 人事室 and your principal by March, not June. Staffing plans, funding applications and recruitment are decided months before the year ends, and silence is almost never a signal about your performance — it usually means nobody thought to tell the foreign teacher about a process conducted in Chinese. Ask specifically about the timeline, the paperwork, and whether your work permit and ARC renew in step with the contract.

If you are leaving

Start six weeks out · 提前六週

The order matters more than the list.

Six weeks: tell the Personnel Office in writing. Ask them for your school's own leaving checklist — most have one, and it will include things this list cannot know about.

Four weeks: income tax. A departing resident generally settles the current year's tax before leaving rather than waiting for the following May. This is the step people discover too late, and it is the one that can block your departure.

Two weeks: NHI withdrawal, any pension or severance entitlement under your contract, and the return of keys, devices and materials. Hand over anything the next teacher will need — your lesson files are worth more to the school than the equipment.

Last week: bank account, ARC, forwarding address. Do not close the bank account until the final salary and any severance have actually landed.

Doing this badly can cause real problems if you ever apply to work in Taiwan again. Doing it well takes about four hours spread over six weeks.

One thing to do this week

Whether you are staying or going, write one paragraph per class describing what they can actually do in English now that they could not do in September — in plain sentences, not bands. If you are staying it becomes your baseline. If you are leaving it is the single most useful thing you can hand to whoever replaces you.
