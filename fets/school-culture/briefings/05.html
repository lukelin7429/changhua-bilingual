The one sentence

If you become aware of a suspected campus gender incident, you must report it within 24 hours — and failing to do so carries a fine of NT$30,000 to 150,000.

That is five times the child-protection penalty, and it applies to the principal, teachers, staff and janitors alike. Destroying or hiding evidence carries the same fine.

What counts as a campus gender incident

Under the Gender Equity Education Act (性別平等教育法, as amended 16 August 2023), a campus gender incident is one where one party is a principal, teacher, staff member, worker or student, and the other party is a student, involving any of:

TypeIn plain terms
Sexual assault
性侵害		As defined by the sexual assault prevention legislation.
Sexual harassment
性騷擾		Unwanted sex- or gender-related conduct that creates a hostile environment, or that is tied to a benefit or disadvantage.
Sexual bullying
性霸凌		Persistent belittling, attacks or threats aimed at someone's sex, sexual characteristics, sexual orientation or gender identity — including between students.
Professional-ethics violation
違反專業倫理		Added in the 2023 amendment. Sex- or gender-related conduct by a principal or staff member that breaches professional ethics — it does not have to reach the threshold of harassment to be actionable.

Note the shape of it: the other party must be a student. That is what makes it a campus gender incident rather than something else — see “If it happens to you” below.

The duty, and what it costs to miss it

ProvisionWhat it says
Article 22A principal, teacher, staff member or worker who becomes aware of a suspected campus gender incident must report it to the school's designated staff — no later than 24 hours.
Article 43Failing to report within 24 hours without legitimate cause: NT$30,000 – 150,000. Forging, altering, destroying or concealing evidence of such an incident: the same.
Article 36The Gender Equity Education Committee (性平會) must complete its investigation within two months, extendable twice by one month each. The committee investigates. You do not.

“Suspected” is the operative word. You are not asked to decide whether it happened, and you do not need the student's permission to report. As with child protection, the clock starts when the first staff member knows and is shared by everyone who knows.

The boundaries that keep you out of all of this

Almost every case involving a well-meaning foreign teacher comes down to one of the six habits below. None of them are about your intentions. The test applied afterwards is how the conduct reasonably appeared — which is why the habits, not the intentions, are what protect you.

  • Stay visible不單獨密閉相處
    Never one-to-one with a student behind a closed door. Door open, blinds up, or move to a corridor or shared space. If a student needs a private conversation, have it somewhere public enough that a passer-by can see you both.
  • No private messaging不私訊學生
    No personal LINE, Instagram or WhatsApp with students. Do not accept student follow requests. If communication is needed, it goes through the homeroom teacher or a school channel where a colleague can see it.
  • No cars, no outings不單獨載送、不私下相約
    Do not drive a student anywhere alone, and do not meet students outside school without the school knowing and a colleague present.
  • Public, brief, age-appropriate touch肢體接觸
    Young children will hug you. Redirect to a high-five, in view of others, without shaming the child. Nothing private, nothing prolonged, nothing out of sight.
  • No remarks about bodies不評論外貌身體
    Not appearance, not weight, not development, not who is dating whom — not even as a compliment, and not even repeating what a student said. This includes remarks about colleagues.
  • No individual gifts or favours不個別餽贈
    What reads as kindness to you can read very differently in a written summary six months later. Give to the class, not to the child.

If a student tells you something

The same four steps as Module 4. Stay and listen without questioning. Do not promise secrecy — say instead that you have to tell one person whose job it is to help. Do not investigate, do not ask other students, and above all do not approach the person the student mentioned. Write down the date, time, place and their words, then tell the designated person the same day.

If the concern involves the school's own principal, the case does not stay inside the school — it goes to the county education authority. A school cannot investigate its own head.

If it happens to you

A campus gender incident requires the other party to be a student. So if you are the person being harassed — by a colleague, a parent, or a student — that route is not the one that covers you. Your protection comes through your employer: go to 人事室 and the principal, ask what procedure applies, and ask for the response in writing. If the school does not act, the county Education Department and the labour authority are both open to you, and 1990 can point you to the right office in English.

Say this plainly, because it is often left unsaid: you are entitled to raise this, and doing so is not a threat to your contract.

One thing to do this week

Open your phone and check: are any current students following you, or you them, on a personal account? If so, remove them this week — quietly, before it is ever a question. It is the single highest-value ninety seconds in this entire module.
