FET Monthly Meeting
June 17, 2026

Changhua's foreign English teachers came together for their monthly meeting. In small group discussions, each team shared the classroom strategies, activities, and tools that have worked for them — from culture and camps to AI-made games, storytelling, and sister-school partnerships. Here are the highlights.

Discussion Highlights 討論重點

What each group shared, in their own words — practical ideas you can adapt for your own classroom.

EG 1

Bringing World Cultures & Festivals into the Classroom

For summer / winter camps and English Club classes

  • Explored how to weave cultural traditions and festivals into lessons for summer and winter camps and English Club classes.
  • Teacher Dan A. shared videos of his middle-grade students playing with a piñata, connecting the Mexican festival and birthday tradition to Filipino culture.
  • Teacher Amelia W. walked through the process of making guacamole, a traditional dish from Mexico and Central and South America.
  • Both activities promote global partnership and cross-cultural understanding in the classroom.
EG 2

Designing Summer & Winter Camps + SUNO AI

Nanxing Elementary School · "Sharing is Caring"

  • Built camps around the UN Sustainable Development Goals: a Winter Camp on "Life Below Water" (SDG 14) and a Summer Camp on "Life on Land" (SDG 15).
  • Design method: decide the final product first (e.g., a musical play, "The Ocean Protectors"), then plan backward from it.
  • Sample summer days — Day 1: animal classification (vertebrates vs. invertebrates; native wildlife like the Formosan rock macaque and pangolin) with clay sculpting; Day 2: diets & food chains with 3D pop-up cards; Day 3: homes & habitats (nests, burrows, dens, hives).
  • Every session threads five pillars: Vocabulary → Sentence Patterns → Science Connection → Creative Activity → Presentation Practice.
  • Introduced SUNO AI (text-to-music) to boost vocabulary retention, model natural pronunciation and intonation, and lower students' anxiety through stress-free, song-based learning.
EG 3

Hosting Classroom Games with GitHub & Gemini

Ready-to-play games for the English Corner

  • Used Google Gemini to generate single-file, browser-based HTML games — no installation needed, and easy to host and share on GitHub.
  • Showcased a finished "Travel Around the World" geography game where students complete challenges and design their own digital boarding pass.
  • Shared an "Everyday English" game for low-pressure practice in the classroom or English Corner.
  • Focused on showing the finished, playable product so colleagues can reuse and adapt resources rather than start from scratch.
  • Positioned GitHub + Gemini games as a practical, low-barrier resource for English Corner enrichment.
EG 4

A Practical Workshop: Making Games with Gemini

Write prompts instead of code

  • A hands-on walkthrough teaching educators to create HTML games with Gemini instead of coding manually.
  • Workflow: open Gemini → write a detailed prompt → let it generate the HTML → paste into Notepad → save as an .html file → open in Chrome and play.
  • Prompt-writing tips: be clear and direct, keep instructions simple, and request big buttons, bright colors, clear text, sound effects, a scoring system, and full-screen display.
  • Always ask Gemini to put everything in one HTML file, tell it to fix any errors, and keep refining until the game matches your vision.
EG 5

Classroom Challenges & Effective Interventions

Real problems, practical responses

  • Tablet distraction (1:1 device setup): temporarily collect a misused iPad, return it to the charging cart until needed, and reinforce responsible-use expectations and consequences.
  • Lessons delivered too fast (especially when moving from junior high to elementary): coordinate pacing with your co-teacher and adjust to students' comprehension level.
  • Limited co-teacher coordination (FET left only a few minutes due to poor time management): discuss scheduling and pacing with co-teachers, and raise it with administrators if it continues.
  • Teasing during group work when a student can't answer: set clear expectations for respectful teamwork, emphasize collaboration and mutual support, and involve the homeroom teacher for added behavioral support.
EG 6

Building Global Connections: ESL Through Sister-School Partnerships

Theories · suggested activities · school events

  • Framed sister-school partnerships to give learners confidence and a real audience for their English.
  • Exchanged student self-introduction videos between Siang Tian Elementary (Taiwan) and Senior Tesoro Academy (Philippines).
  • Spotlighted "English Day" as a sister-school program: students write postcards, make short videos, hold real-life conversations, and compare English Day in Taiwan vs. the partner school.
  • Compared signature events — Taiwan's Sports Day (relays, tug-of-war, novelty games, parent and teacher participation) and the Philippines' Foundation Day & Intramurals (cultural shows, sports fests, parades).
  • Suggested cross-school activities: virtual vocabulary lessons, a mock sports day, cultural-share sessions, live watch parties, and cultural-exchange blogs via Google Classroom.
JG 1

English Club: Movie Watching & Voice Dubbing

Building speaking confidence through movies

  • An English Club activity that builds speaking and listening through movies and voice acting.
  • Steps: watch an engaging English movie or scene → answer comprehension questions on a worksheet → read the script aloud together → record (dub) their own voices over the characters.
  • Goals: check understanding and new vocabulary, practice clear pronunciation, pacing, and intonation, and build speaking confidence and fluency in a fun, low-pressure way.
JG 2

Storytelling Frameworks & a Teacher-Led Digital Library

Multi-sensory reading beyond rote

  • Explored multi-sensory storybook teaching that goes beyond rote reading to build conceptual mastery.
  • Wordless / sequential-illustration books: with no pre-written text, students become the narrators — interpreting illustrations, discussing spontaneously, and turning visual clues into English sentences (building critical thinking).
  • Concept-integrated storytelling: teach background knowledge first (e.g., habitats, animal adaptations), then apply it to understand and discuss the story (sample units on forests, grasslands, deserts, farms, oceans, and "what if animals wore clothes").
  • Proposed a district-wide, open-access cloud Digital Library: foreign teachers scan, categorize, and upload useful storybooks and lesson guides to share resources with one another.
